# Content and Editorial Policy

**Status:** baseline policy proposal; historian, community, and legal review required before public launch.

## 1. Purpose

WorldHeritage publishes accessible historical learning material that is evidence-led, transparent about uncertainty, respectful of affected communities, and accountable to correction. The platform distinguishes evidence from interpretation and does not present editorial confidence as universal scholarly consensus.

## 2. Evidence and citations

- Link each material factual claim to a source at the narrowest practical level (claim, paragraph, or revision). A source list without a clear relation to claims is insufficient for contested or consequential claims.
- Record enough bibliographic information to locate a source: creator/author, title, publisher/collection, date where known, stable URL or identifier, access date for mutable web sources, and page/folio/section locator when available.
- Identify source type and evidentiary role (primary source, peer-reviewed scholarship, reference work, oral history, archaeological evidence, institutional record, etc.) without assuming a category alone proves reliability.
- Preserve the source's context, provenance, limitations, and relevant translation/edition details. Cite translations when quoted or relied upon.
- Never invent sources, quotations, page numbers, citations, or corroboration. If evidence cannot be found, state that clearly and do not publish a confident factual claim as verified.

## 3. Epistemic labels

Editors should visibly distinguish:

- **Well-supported / verified:** supported by credible, relevant evidence and appropriate review. “Verified” means reviewed against cited evidence, not absolute certainty.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** an interpretation of evidence; identify the interpretive nature and cite representative scholarship.
- **Disputed:** credible sources or scholarly communities materially disagree. Summarize significant views fairly, attribute them, and explain the nature of disagreement; do not manufacture false balance.
- **Uncertain / unresolved:** evidence is incomplete, ambiguous, or insufficient. Say what is unknown and why where possible.

These are editorial labels, not claims that every field can be settled by a single authority. Avoid presenting denialism, propaganda, or unsupported assertions as equivalent to well-supported scholarship.

## 4. Historical dates

Retain source wording and specify calendar and precision. Use “circa/around,” a range, decade, or century when evidence supports only that degree of precision. Record whether a date is exact, approximate, disputed, or unknown. Explain BCE/CE, regnal years, calendar conversion, or alternate chronologies where material. Never silently convert an uncertain date into an exact timestamp.

## 5. Review and publication

1. Author creates a sourced draft and declares relevant affiliations/conflicts.
2. Submission captures an immutable revision.
3. A qualified reviewer checks source relevance, claim support, uncertainty labels, respectful framing, translation quality where applicable, and rights.
4. Reviewer records approve, request changes, or reject with rationale. Authors cannot approve their own work.
5. An authorized Country Editor (within assigned scope) or Super Admin publishes only the approved revision.
6. Material later edits create a new revision and review. Corrections retain a visible history and date.

Editorial queue access and publication are server-authorized, scope-limited, and audited. Urgent safety/legal removal may temporarily restrict access under a documented process, with later review and a retained internal record.

## 6. Somali and English localization

Treat Somali and English as first-class editorial locales. Keep named entities and transliterations consistent while retaining meaningful local forms and variants. A translator should receive the source revision and context; reviewers should check meaning, names, dates, citations, and culturally specific terminology. Mark machine-assisted translations as unreviewed until a qualified human has reviewed them. Missing translations must be explicit; fallback must identify the displayed locale.

## 7. Respect, sensitive history, and living communities

Use precise, humane language, especially for colonialism, violence, displacement, religion, identity, and contested sovereignty. Attribute historically loaded labels to their source/context. Avoid collective blame, dehumanization, sensationalism, and presenting modern political borders as timeless. Seek qualified regional/contextual review for material affecting living communities; respect privacy and informed consent for contemporary oral-history contributors. Minimize personal data and do not publish sensitive identifying information without a documented basis and safeguards.

## 8. Copyright, licensing, and media

Facts are not a license to copy protected expression. Do not scrape or republish copyrighted text, images, audio, video, or digitized archival material without authorization or a clearly applicable license/exception reviewed for the applicable jurisdiction. Record creator, rights holder, license/permission, attribution text, and permitted uses. Link to lawful sources where republication rights are unclear. Require documented rights review before storing or serving media. Respect takedown and rights-holder contact processes established before launch.

## 9. Corrections, reports, and appeals

Provide a report route for factual errors, missing context, translation issues, harmful framing, and rights concerns. Triage by severity and evidence; acknowledge where a response channel exists; preserve the prior published revision and correction note unless privacy, safety, or law requires removal. Record what changed, why, who reviewed it, and when. Provide an appeal route for rejected submissions and define response targets before launch.

## 10. Prohibited editorial practices

Fabricated citations or evidence; unattributed copying; disguising opinion as established fact; silently changing an approved text; suppressing material uncertainty; publishing unreviewed contributor claims as authoritative; and unauthorized scraping or media republication.

## 11. Governance questions before launch

Define reviewer qualification and conflict standards, source evaluation guidance by discipline, community consultation, appeals/takedown timing, content retention, moderation escalation, and jurisdiction-specific copyright/privacy review. This policy is a starting framework and not legal advice.
